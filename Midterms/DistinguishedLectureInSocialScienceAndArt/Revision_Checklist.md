# DLSSA: Revision Checklist

Scope: eight supplied decks, 103 slides, weeks 1-7. This is a study checklist, not an official list of exam questions.

For each item: define it without notes, give an example, and explain a common confusion. Revisit items you can recognize but cannot apply.

## 1. Introduction and critical thinking

**Notes:** [Week 1](notes/01_Week1_CriticalThinking.md).

- [ ] Distinguish social science, humanities and arts.
- [ ] Explain why these subjects matter for computing and engineering.
- [ ] Define critical thinking, logic and opinion in the lecture's usage.
- [ ] Explain the authority/obedience discussion without inventing experimental details.
- [ ] Compare dualism, relativism and commitment.
- [ ] Identify analytical, inquiry, communication and collaborative habits.
- [ ] Reproduce Experience, Interpretation, Analysis in order.
- [ ] Separate an observation from an unsupported interpretation.
- [ ] Distinguish resistance from narrow-mindedness.
- [ ] Recognize rationalization, doublethink, dissonance and stress as listed barriers.

## 2. Reason and emotion

**Notes:** [Week 2](notes/02_Week2_Reason_Emotion.md).

- [ ] Define reason, emotional intelligence and empathy.
- [ ] Name the historical views of reason summarized by the lecture.
- [ ] Explain why a demographic stereotype is not an evaluation of someone's reasoning.
- [ ] Treat the dreams strategy as a possible source of ideas requiring checking.
- [ ] Give a constructive and a harmful role for emotion.
- [ ] Explain how to integrate emotion with evidence-based judgment.
- [ ] Discuss AI assistance and its risks without assuming performance proves consciousness.
- [ ] Distinguish faith, fideism, religious rationalism, atheism and agnosticism.
- [ ] Use critical rationalism in the specific sense given by this lecture.

## 3. Knowledge and evidence

**Notes:** [Week 3](notes/03_Week3_Knowledge_Evidence_Biases.md).

- [ ] Contrast rationalism, empiricism and the mind's structuring role.
- [ ] Define knowledge and evidence.
- [ ] Distinguish direct experience, hearsay, anecdote and false memory.
- [ ] Recall expert education, experience, reputation and accomplishments.
- [ ] Explain why credibility does not guarantee infallibility.
- [ ] Identify confirmation bias and deliberately seek counterevidence.
- [ ] Recognize perceptual, random-pattern, memorable-events and probability errors.
- [ ] Distinguish self-serving bias from a self-fulfilling prophecy.
- [ ] Distinguish stereotyping, in-group/out-group thinking, groupthink and diffusion of responsibility.

## 4. Fallacies

**Notes:** [Week 4](notes/04_Week4_InformalFallacies.md) and [reference](Fallacy_Reference.md).

- [ ] Distinguish formal from informal fallacies.
- [ ] Name all four ambiguity fallacies.
- [ ] Name all eight relevance fallacies.
- [ ] Name all seven unwarranted-assumption fallacies.
- [ ] Explain each one using its failed inference and a new example.
- [ ] Distinguish equivocation/amphiboly, division/hasty generalization and straw man/red herring.
- [ ] Distinguish circular support from a loaded question.
- [ ] Repair an argument without assuming its conclusion must be false.

## 5. Arguments

**Notes:** [Week 5](notes/05_Week5_Arguments.md).

- [ ] Turn a broad topic into a clearly framed issue.
- [ ] Explain the lecture's rhetoric/argumentation contrast.
- [ ] Identify propositions, premises and conclusions.
- [ ] Classify descriptive, prescriptive, analogical and definitional premises.
- [ ] Distinguish argument, explanation and conditional.
- [ ] Use indicators as clues and reconstruct unstated assumptions.
- [ ] Evaluate clarity, credibility, relevance, completeness and soundness.
- [ ] Explain the hidden causal assumption in the front-row example.
- [ ] Reproduce the eight argument-construction steps.

## 6. Advertising and media

**Notes:** [Week 6.1](notes/06_Week6_Marketing_Advertising.md), [Week 6.2](notes/07_Week6_MassMedia.md).

- [ ] Distinguish marketing surveys, observation and experimentation.
- [ ] Define strategic plan and the three advertising purposes.
- [ ] Recognize product placement and its persuasive role.
- [ ] Distinguish snob appeal from popular appeal.
- [ ] Explain euphemism, hyperbole and analogy.
- [ ] Check the evidence behind statistics and testimonials.
- [ ] Define mass media and media literacy.
- [ ] Recall all six scientific-reporting checks.
- [ ] Analyze creator, purpose, attention techniques, values and viewpoints.
- [ ] Include audience reactions, different interpretations, reasoning, facts and effects.
- [ ] Explain why funding deserves scrutiny but does not by itself prove a result false.

## 7. Science

**Notes:** [Week 7](notes/08_Week7_Science.md).

- [ ] Define science and the five assumptions named in the lecture.
- [ ] Explain limitations of observation and causal inference.
- [ ] Compare the four science/religion positions.
- [ ] State the five scientific-method steps.
- [ ] Apply relevance, consistency, simplicity, testability/falsifiability and prediction.
- [ ] Explain pseudoscience using a reasoning or testing failure.
- [ ] Recognize experimental, correlational, survey, ethnographic, grounded-theory, narrative, action and mixed-method research.
- [ ] Distinguish collection ethics from reporting ethics.
- [ ] Explain publish-or-perish pressure and the neutrality concern.
- [ ] Distinguish normal science, paradigm and scientific revolution.

- [ ] Complete [set 1](MidtermSamples/Midterm_Practice_1.md) before reading its answers.
