## 3. Authority and independent judgment (slides 10-12)

The lecture introduces Eichmann's trial, the Milgram experiment and the Stanford Prison experiment to ask about obedience, authority and responsibility.

The explicit takeaway on slide 12 is that many people uncritically follow authority. The decks do not supply experimental procedures, numerical results or methodological evaluations. Do not invent these details from the slide titles.

**Study application:** a manager's instruction is not enough to establish that an action is justified. Examine the reason, evidence and consequences while taking responsibility for your judgment.

## 4. Critical thinking, logic and opinion (slides 13-14)

| Term | Meaning used in the lecture |
|---|---|
| Critical thinking | Everyday skills needed for intellectual and personal development, emphasizing how to think |
| Logic | Study of methods and principles that distinguish good arguments from poor ones |
| Opinion | A belief based solely on personal feelings rather than reason or facts |

**Study clarification:** opinion has a broader everyday meaning and can describe an evidence-informed judgment. For a course definition, reproduce the narrower usage above and distinguish it from a justified claim.

Critical thinking requires asking what supports a claim, considering alternatives and being willing to revise it. Criticism alone does not establish that a belief is false.

## 5. Cognitive development (slide 15)

| Stage | Lecture description | Study example |
|---|---|---|
| Dualism | Right and wrong answers exist and authorities know the right ones | Accept a claim solely because a lecturer says it |
| Relativism | When authorities lack an answer, everyone has an opinion and there are no right or wrong answers | Treat all conflicting claims as equally justified |
| Commitment | Avoid blindly following or opposing authority | Adopt a position after examining evidence and remain open to revision |

Commitment involves responsible judgment under uncertainty. It does not mean certainty or loyalty to one's first belief.

## 6. Characteristics of a critical thinker (slides 16-17)

- Analytical skills and effective communication.
- Research and inquiry skills.
- Flexibility and tolerance of ambiguity.
- Open-minded skepticism, associated in the slide with Descartes' method of doubt.
- Creative problem solving.
- Attention, mindfulness and curiosity.
- Collaborative learning.

**Study example:** ask a classmate for their strongest objection and revise a weak premise. This uses inquiry, communication and openness together.

## 7. Three-tier model (slide 19)

The diagram's order is **Experience, Interpretation, Analysis**. Its labels are in a diagram and can be missed by simple text extraction.

| Tier | Study clarification | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | What happened or was perceived | A friend did not reply today |
| Interpretation | Meaning assigned to that event | They must be angry with me |
| Analysis | Evaluate the interpretation and alternatives | They could be busy, offline or upset. What evidence distinguishes these? |

The example and explanatory wording are study additions. The slide itself supplies the three labels and their order.

## 8. Resistance and narrow-mindedness (slides 20-21)

**Resistance:** habitual use of immature defense mechanisms when worldviews are challenged. The slide lists avoidance, anger, cliches, denial, ignorance, conformity, struggling and distractions.

**Narrow-mindedness:** rigid beliefs that interfere with critical analysis. The lecture lists absolutism, fear of challenge, egocentrism, ethnocentrism and anthropocentrism.

Study glosses for the listed terms:

| Term | Useful distinction |
|---|---|
| Absolutism | Treat a position as beyond question |
| Egocentrism | Center one's own perspective and interests |
| Ethnocentrism | Judge other cultures through the assumed superiority of one's own |
| Anthropocentrism | Center human interests or perspectives |

These glosses explain named items. The slide does not provide their full definitions.

## 9. Other barriers (slide 22)

The slide names rationalization/doublethink, cognitive/social dissonance and stress without elaboration.

**Study clarifications:** rationalization supplies a convenient justification for a belief or action. Doublethink involves holding incompatible beliefs without adequately addressing the conflict. Cognitive dissonance concerns tension between inconsistent beliefs or behavior. Social dissonance here can be studied as tension between one's views and social expectations, but no formal definition is supplied by the deck.

**Study example:** after ignoring evidence, saying "I did not need to check because I am always careful" protects self-image instead of evaluating the decision.
