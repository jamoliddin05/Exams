# Distinguished Lecture in Social Science and Art: Midterm Preparation

Preparation for **DLSSA weeks 1-7**, based on all eight supplied lecture decks: **103 slides**. The syllabus in `DLSSA 1.pptx` s6-7 places the offline midterm in week 8, covering the Critical Thinking chapters from weeks 1-7 and carrying 30% of the course grade. These are statements in the supplied syllabus, not a newly verified exam announcement.

## 1. Start here

1. Read the lecture notes in order, especially the definitions and worked examples.
2. Learn the [fallacy reference](Fallacy_Reference.md): identify the faulty inference and explain it in your own words.
3. Attempt the two practice sets before opening their answer sections.
4. Use the [revision checklist](Revision_Checklist.md) to check recall and application.

Notes follow the repository format: one file per deck, numbered sections, plain slide references, worked study examples and **Exam focus**. All navigation links open Markdown. The notes are in English, matching the lectures and the other course sections.

## 4. Skills to practice across topics

| Task | What a useful answer contains |
|---|---|
| Define a concept | Its meaning and one distinguishing feature |
| Distinguish concepts | A difference plus an example that makes the difference visible |
| Analyze an argument | Issue, conclusion, premises, hidden assumptions, evaluation and a justified verdict |
| Identify a fallacy | Name, specific wording, missing logical connection and a repair |
| Evaluate evidence | Source, relevant expertise, method, limitations, alternatives and counterevidence |
| Evaluate a media message | Creator, purpose, attention techniques, values, audience reactions, factual support and effects |
| Evaluate a hypothesis | Relevance, consistency, simplicity, testability/falsifiability and prediction |

These are study priorities derived from the lecture content. There is no past-paper frequency evidence for ranking topics.

## 5. Source boundaries and ambiguities

- **Week 6 mismatch:** W1 s6 names Marketing, Mass Media and Politics under Chapter 11. The actual decks label marketing Chapter 10 and media Chapter 11. Neither supplied deck contains a separate politics lecture. The notes follow the deck titles and flag the gap.
- **Brief slides:** several concepts are only named. Extra definitions, explanations and all newly constructed examples are marked **study clarification** or **study example**. They are not quotations or additional instructor content.
- **External material:** slides link videos, articles and an optical-illusion site. Their contents were not supplied and are not reconstructed here. Eichmann, Milgram, Stanford Prison, Peter Reilly, Shannon Townsend and Lincoln-Douglas appear as discussion prompts, not fully documented cases in these decks.
- **Course terminology:** retain the lecture's fallacy categories, simplified definition of opinion, faith-related vocabulary and broad argument-evaluation use of soundness. More precise distinctions are explicitly labeled in the notes.
- **W2 s6 typo:** a sentence on women's reasoning contradicts its surrounding bullets. The notes explain the conflict rather than repeating it as a factual claim.
- **Science:** W7 s7 places scientific theory in parentheses beside initial hypothesis. The notes avoid treating every tentative hypothesis as an established theory. W7 s5 names quantum mechanics without explaining it.
- **Historical material:** claims and images in the slides are course material, not a current scientific/news update. The introductory climate illustration is not used as independent evidence about a present event.
- **Access:** this package is grounded in the eight uploaded files. The eClass course contents, announcements, quizzes and linked readings were not independently accessed. Updates outside these files may change the exam scope.
