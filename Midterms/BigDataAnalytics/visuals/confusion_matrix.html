Confusion Matrix by Hand

Both models in this problem score above 99% accuracy. One of them is excellent. The other gets 7 of its 10 fraud alerts wrong. The matrix shows the difference that the accuracy number hides.

How to read the grid

Rows are what really happened. Columns are what the model said. Each of the four boxes counts the cases with one combination. The first word of the name tells you if the model was right, and the second word tells you what it said.

All
  • TPTrue Positive. Correct, and the model said yes.
  • FNFalse Negative. Wrong, and the model said no. A real yes slipped through.
  • FPFalse Positive. Wrong, and the model said yes. A false alarm.
  • TNTrue Negative. Correct, and the model said no.

The diagonal (TP and TN) holds the correct answers. The row totals P and N count what really happened. The column totals P′ and N′ count what the model said.

Three metrics, three slices of the grid

Each metric is a fraction built from part of the grid. The filled cells go on top of the fraction. The outlined cells go on the bottom.

Accuracy

yesno yesTPFN noFPTN
(TP + TN) / All Out of everything, how much did the model get right? Uses the diagonal over the whole grid.

Precision

yesno yesTPFN noFPTN
TP / (TP + FP) When the model says yes, how often is it right? Uses only the predicted-yes column.

Recall

yesno yesTPFN noFPTN
TP / (TP + FN) Of all the real yeses, how many did the model catch? Uses only the actual-yes row.

Part (a) · 8 pts

M1: catching credit card fraud

What the question gives you

  • 1000 transactions in the test data
  • 5 of them are actually fraud (actual yes, the row total P)
  • M1 flagged 10 as fraud (predicted yes, the column total P′)
  • 3 of those flags are real fraud (TP)

Why 99.1% accuracy means little here. Only 5 of the 1000 dots are fraud, so the 988 grey dots make the score look great whatever the model does with the rare class. A model that never flags anything does even better on accuracy.

Part (b) · 8 pts

M2: approving loans

What the question gives you

  • 1000 loan reviews in the test data
  • 502 are actual approvals (actual yes, P)
  • M2 predicted 500 approvals (predicted yes, P′)
  • 499 of those are real approvals (TP)

Here the high accuracy is real. The classes are about half and half, so the score cannot hide behind a huge grey majority. Precision and recall are both above 99% too.

M1 vs M2 side by side

The accuracy bars are almost the same length. Precision and recall show how different the two models are.

What to write on the paper

(a) M1, positive = Fraud
                   Pred Yes   Pred No   Total
    Actual Yes        3          2         5
    Actual No         7        988       995
    Total            10        990      1000

    TP = 3,  FP = 10 − 3 = 7,  FN = 5 − 3 = 2,  TN = 1000 − 3 − 7 − 2 = 988
    Accuracy  = (3 + 988) / 1000 = 99.1%
    Precision = 3 / 10           = 30%
    Recall    = 3 / 5            = 60%

(b) M2, positive = Loan approved
                   Pred Yes   Pred No   Total
    Actual Yes      499          3       502
    Actual No         1        497       498
    Total           500        500      1000

    TP = 499,  FP = 500 − 499 = 1,  FN = 502 − 499 = 3,  TN = 1000 − 499 − 1 − 3 = 497
    Accuracy  = (499 + 497) / 1000 = 99.6%
    Precision = 499 / 500          = 99.8%
    Recall    = 499 / 502          = 99.4%
